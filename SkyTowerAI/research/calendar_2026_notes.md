## TASK 3 — What changed between 2018–19 and 2026 (playbook-transfer risk)

Structural/schedule changes (verified): (1) RBA regime is different in kind: 2018–19 had 11 meetings/yr (first Tuesday of month, 14:30, no regular presser, SMP on a separate Friday); since 2024 there are 8 two-day meetings with the decision at 14:30 on day 2 (day of week varies), a press conference every time at 15:30, and since 2025 the SMP drops simultaneously with the Feb/May/Aug/Nov decisions — so an "RBA day" now has two-plus information events and old first-Tuesday seasonality is void ([RBA mr-25-02](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2025/mr-25-02.html)). (2) AU Retail Sales m/m — the exact series your 2018–19 playbooks used — no longer exists: the Retail Business Survey/Retail Trade publication ceased after June-2025 data, replaced by the bank-transaction-based Monthly Household Spending Indicator with broader coverage and different volatility; there is no like-for-like mapping ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/cessation-retail-business-survey-and-retail-trade-publication)). (3) AU CPI: in 2018–19 inflation was a single quarterly q/q bomb; since 26 Nov 2025 a complete Monthly CPI (last Wednesday of the month) is the primary headline measure, with legacy quarterly series only until ~mid-2027 — per-event impact is diluted across 12 releases and the RBA now reads the monthly ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-statements/australias-first-complete-monthly-cpi-out-tomorrow), [RBA technical note](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/technical-notes/2025/the-transition-to-a-complete-monthly-cpi.html)). (4) BoC decisions are now 09:45 ET (verified for all of 2026); in 2018–19 they were 10:00 ET, so raw historical reaction timestamps are offset 15 minutes (the 2023 change date itself: from records, not re-verified). (5) Stable anchors that DO transfer: RBNZ still 7 decisions/yr at 14:00 NZT; Stats NZ still 10:45 NZT (CPI/GDP/HLFS/retail all unchanged, and the quarterly NZ Retail Trade Survey still exists — Mar-2026 quarter released 22 May 2026); StatCan still 08:30 ET; BLS CPI/NFP still 08:30 ET; FOMC still 8×/yr at 14:00/14:30 ET. (6) Nordics: Riksbank now runs ~8 meetings/yr at 09:30 (repo rate renamed "policy rate" in 2022; 2018–19 had fewer meetings — count unverified), Norges 8×/yr at 10:00.

Regime differences (verified from 2026 reporting, and the bigger transfer risk): 2019 was a synchronized global EASING cycle in a low-inflation, low-vol world — every Task-2 surprise above is a dovish-cut or growth-miss story. Mid-2026 is the opposite: an energy-shock, inflation-re-acceleration environment (Middle East conflict; oil moves around the partial reopening of the Strait of Hormuz) in which the Fed is on a hawkish hold at 3.50–3.75% with the June-2026 dot plot flipping to a projected HIKE under new chair Warsh ([Fed statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260617a.htm), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/17/fed-interest-rate-decision-june-2026.html)); the RBNZ delivered a surprise 25bp HIKE to 2.50% on 8 Jul 2026 after a 3-3 split hold in May, guiding toward 2.75–3.00% by end-2026 ([RBNZ](https://www.rbnz.govt.nz/news-and-events/news/2026/07/ocr-increased-to-2-50-to-return-inflation-to-2-percent), [Westpac](https://www.westpaciq.com.au/economics/2026/07/rbnz-monetary-policy-review-july-2026)); the RBA sits at 4.35% after +75bp of 2026 hikes with a hawkish pause ([SBS live blog](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/live-blog/rba-june-2026-interest-rates-decision-live/ntpgs9xc6)); the BoC holds at 2.25% (5th straight hold, decision on 15 Jul 2026 — tomorrow — with MPR) citing energy prices and US trade-policy uncertainty ([BoC](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2026/06/fad-press-release-2026-06-10/)); Norges is at 4.25% after a May-2026 hike, Riksbank at 1.75%. Practical implication: 2018–19 playbooks encode dovish-surprise asymmetry (misses punished harder than beats, cuts as the surprise direction); in 2026 the tail risk is hawkish (surprise hikes, hot CPI), so direction-conditional reaction sizes should be re-weighted rather than copied, and central-bank days now carry second-leg risk from pressers (RBA 15:30 local, FOMC 14:30 ET, RBNZ MPS 15:00 NZT).
