## 1. Core BTMM market model

**Premise.** Price is moved by deliberate institutional order flow, not sentiment. The "market maker" (MM) is best modeled as a single very large counterparty that sits on the other side of most retail transactions. Moving price is expensive (the notes claim ~10,000 lots per pip), so every engineered move exists to make later profit: entice retail into positions, then reverse price so retail exits at a loss to the MM's benefit. The MM's constraints: finite capital (it must periodically close positions and rebalance — the stated cause of "aggressive pullbacks that seem to occur out of nowhere") and an externally bounded daily range (the notes claim IMF-limited, in practice ~ADR, up to ~200 pips/day on most pairs). Brokers/dealers add micro-tools: requotes, triggering all stops in a price range, varying the spread (given as the reason scalping fails), throwing price spikes to run stops ("they know where the stops are"), and finishing off traders in margin trouble.

**Intraday cycle (the daily template).** (a) *Accumulation*: begins at the 17:00 ET daily reset, the quiet "Dharma" period after NY close — circular trading between banks makes price oscillate in a range; the range then widens to trigger breakout orders on both sides. (b) *Stop hunt / manipulation*: between roughly 1:00–4:00 AM ET price is deliberately pushed out of the Asian range — typically 25–50 pips beyond it, in 3 pushes, the final push often "just a tap" leaving a pin/wick — creating the day's high or low (HOD/LOD) in the *opposite* direction to the day's true move. (c) *Distribution / true trend*: a slower trend in ~3 waves runs against the faked direction toward the opposite extreme of the day, usually reached in the NY session (NYC reversal), after which price drifts back toward the middle and re-enters accumulation.

**3-day cycle and levels.** The same shape repeats at a higher degree: from a Peak Formation High, price steps down through Level I → Level II → Level III, each level followed by a consolidation whose job is to run stops in both directions (20–30 pip swings). Levels I and II are orderly ("1-2-3 stop hunt" behavior, ~75 pips each from peak to end-of-day consolidation); Level III is deliberately choppy and wide — institutional profit-taking and the seed of the next accumulation — then a Peak Formation Low forms and the cycle inverts. Mnemonics: "after three days of drop the market must chop"; "after three days of rise the market needs more guys." Level 1 is MM-driven (fast), Level 2 is retail/emotion-driven (smaller moves), Level 3 sees the MM return with the heaviest volume.

**Weekly cycle.** Seen on M15–H4 (a daily chart is "too slow"): the week opens (Sun/Mon) with traders already trapped from the weekend and a smallish range that draws positions one way; a significantly larger opposite move produces the midweek high/low (Tue–Wed); a reversal then runs into a closing consolidation. Manipulation intensity clusters at the beginning/end of the day, session, week, and quarter. "The patterns are the same no matter the timeframe," and reversals across timeframes tend to synchronize.

**Two trends.** The *technical trend* is what indicators and trendlines show retail; the *true trend* is what the MM intends, revealed only after the stop hunt. Everything in the method is about locating the current position in the count (intraday, 3-day, weekly) and only trading at the reversal seams — with the MM, against the crowd.

## 2. Everything the PDF says about news events

The document treats scheduled news as an MM tool, not a trading venue. Every news-related statement in the PDF:

1. **News as a panic instrument (p.9).** News releases are listed alongside quick moves, spike candles and "inexplicable" price behaviour as deliberate devices to make traders emotional and irrational.
2. **News/sentiment as cover and trap (p.11).** MMs "take advantage of sentiment" generated by news; immediate post-news price movements are sometimes designed to *cover up* the MM's own repositioning. The rumour mill builds a public expectation of direction; it is common for news to be pessimistic on a currency and for the currency to then rise — "but usually after people have been trapped in line with the sentiment." This is the PDF's clearest statement that the pre/around-news consensus move is the trap and the true move comes after — the closest textual support for fading the pre-release direction.
3. **News spike candles are not entries (pp.36–37).** A news spike "pushes up very quickly and then down quickly or vice versa." Straddle orders (pending orders both sides) appear to work on demo but fail live: fills are pushed to the outer extremity of the spike (worst possible fill), then price snaps back and stops the trader out on the other side. Verbatim sentiment: news candles are "a means for banks, brokers and dealers to grab your money." Implication for automation: entry *at* the spike is adversarial territory (slippage/fill manipulation); any news-fade must control for fill quality and spread.
4. **Daily routine (p.56).** First act of the day: check the calendar at Forex Factory. "News disrupts the technicals" and should be avoided while learning; news "can be used to help the MM achieve their targets." Do not hold a position into upcoming news. Two hard avoidances: **never trade Non-Farm Payrolls**, and **avoid trading while the Fed chair is speaking**. However — explicitly — experienced traders "will be able to identify News setups," i.e., the method acknowledges tradable news structures at the expert level without spelling them out.
5. **Checklist item (p.57).** "Out of news time, confirmed by Forex Factory by at least 1 hour (liquidity dries up during that time)" — a 60-minute pre-news exclusion window is the codified rule, with thinning liquidity given as the reason.
6. **News moves the levels (p.58).** "News releases should not be directly traded but can be understood as a method for the MM to finish or move their levels." I.e., releases are used to complete a stop hunt, jump price to the next level of the 3-day cycle, or reach ADR/pivot targets — so the *location* of price relative to levels going into a release predicts what the release will be used for.
7. **Pre-NFP behaviour (p.60, Straightaway trade).** "Leading up to non-farm payroll a trap move might occur as much as 2 days earlier followed by extended consolidation." The manipulation (trap) leg can be executed days *before* a major release, with the release itself launching the true trend directly out of consolidation with no fresh stop hunt.
8. **Spread manipulation around reversals (pp.25, 27; broker tools p.11).** At the HOD/LOD (the manipulation extreme) the spread is deliberately "opened up by a few pips" so orders fill outside normal bounds and positions start negative; the same widening is available to brokers at any time "when it is an advantage to them." Combined with the stop-run pin ("throw a spike" over prior highs/lows with an enlarged spread, then pull away, p.41), this is the PDF's account of the stop-run wicks ("wycinki") the team observes around releases.
9. **Timing caveat (p.61).** The HOD/LOD-creating manipulation is usually a London-open phenomenon, but responsibility can be handed to the Asian or US "office," so the pattern should be read structurally rather than by clock alone — relevant to news at non-London hours.

**Relation to the team's edge (interpretation, not in the PDF):** the PDF never states "the last M1 candle(s) before a release move opposite to the true post-release direction." Its stop-hunt template is session-anchored (1–4 AM ET), not release-anchored. But the team's rule is a direct transposition of the PDF's core mechanic — pre-move = manipulation/trap, expect reversal into the true trend, expect terminal wicks and spread games — onto the release timestamp, and item (2) above ("trapped in line with the sentiment" before the opposite move) plus item (6) (news finishes/moves levels) are consistent with it. The PDF's own preference would be: don't enter *on* the spike; enter on the confirmed reversal structure (second leg / M-W logic) with wide-spread-aware fills.

## 3. Mechanical rules usable in an automated system

### Sessions and timing (as written, 2011-era; verify offsets in UTC per current DST)
- Daily high/low reset: **17:00 ET**. Sessions: Asia 00:30–07:00 GMT; Europe 07:30–13:00 GMT; US 13:30–20:30 GMT; "gap" (handover, quiet) periods 07:00–07:30 GMT, 13:00–13:30 GMT, 20:30–00:30 GMT. Equities open 09:30 ET — where to start watching for the NY reversal.
- Stop hunt that defines HOD/LOD: **01:00–04:00 AM ET**. Best trading windows: **01:00–05:00 ET** (Asia→London) and **08:00–11:00 ET** (NY). Setups form in the last hour of the Asian range or first 2–3 hours of the European session.
- Weekly: cycle starts Sunday; midweek reversal expected Tue–Wed; best days Wed/Thu/Fri; Sun/Mon target only ~40–50 pips. Mid-December to mid-January: choppy, avoid. On US holidays the Asian session becomes the active one.
- Market has exactly 3 states to classify: consolidation/accumulation (anticipate a stop hunt) → stop hunt (wait for the signal) → trend (trade and count the levels).

### Levels, zones, filters
- **Asian range filter:** ideal accumulation channel is **≤ 50 pips** (narrower = better, "wound-up spring").
- **Trading zone (stop-hunt zone):** **25–50 pips beyond the Asian range** — chosen because retail stops sit 25–50 pips behind entries. Slower pairs (CAD) shade smaller; volatile pairs (GBPJPY) larger. Excursion arrives in **3 pushes** (sizes vary, e.g. 25 + 20 + 5–7 pips), final push frequently a mere tap/pin.
- **Extended stop hunt:** if volume accumulated is insufficient, price is pushed a further ~25–50 pips (again ~3 pushes) in the technical-trend direction; a failed first reversal usually means one more 25-pip extension (a third extension is rare).
- **Previous day HOD/LOD:** expect spike-through-and-close-back-inside (stop trigger + inducement, often with widened spread) or an approach-and-fail; bounce most likely near London open. A close back inside the prior high/low invalidates the breakout, not the reversal.
- **ADR high/low** (2-week average range) projected from the day's extremes: reversal magnet, strongest in confluence with pivots or EMA 50/200. NY-reversal box: NY open + ~3 hours; probability rises further if the ADR extreme falls inside that box.
- **Pivots (classic daily, with midpoints M1–M4):** previous daily candle red → expect an "M1/M3 day"; green → "M2/M4 day". HOD gravitates to M3/M4, LOD to M1/M2; these usually land 25–50 pips beyond the Asian range. Unreliable after an abnormally large/small prior day; combine with ADR.
- **Validity rule:** a long remains valid while price holds above the reference low (mirror for shorts); HOD/LOD truly broken (closed beyond) = setup invalid.

### Indicators (exact settings given in the PDF)
- **EMAs 5, 13, 50, 200** (the only numeric indicator settings in the document): 5/13 = signal pair (crossover = trigger/confirmation); 50 = "balance line," intraday trend; 200 = "home base," higher trend — the 200 on M15 proxies the 50 on H1. EMAs confirm *after* the pattern; at Level III they point the wrong way by construction; widely separated EMAs at Level III = trend about to collapse. EMA-5 can be used to denoise the count.
- **RSI** (no period stated): close-based, so it filters manipulation wicks — the divergence detector against spike-extended price highs/lows; baseline cross = momentum shift; regime bands: uptrend RSI 80–40, downtrend 60–20; expect M/W shapes in RSI at reversals.
- **TDI (Traders Dynamic Index)** (components described, no numbers): RSI line + signal line + dynamic market baseline + **volatility bands = Bollinger bands applied to the RSI, not to price**. Key signals: bands tight during Asian consolidation; RSI breaking out of the bands while price sits 25–50 pips beyond the Asian range = stop hunt in progress; **band re-containment after a breakout = stop hunt exhausted, reversal imminent** ("Shark Fin"); then RSI×signal cross, then baseline cross ("blood in the water") as second entry, opposite-band break during the trend run as third. Exit when RSI reaches the opposite band and re-crosses the signal line. Note for the team: the PDF contains **no price-Bollinger settings**; a price-BB confirmation is the team's own overlay — the PDF's analogue is the TDI band logic.
- **Candles** (context-dependent — meaningful only at HOD/LOD/level extremes, never mid-trend): spike/"Empire State" candles (emotion bait, first leg of reversals and Level III), spinning tops/hammers/inverted hammers, dojis, evening/morning star (= a 45-minute railroad track), **RRT (railroad tracks)** = compressed M/W, **high test / low test** = reversal pattern at yesterday's high/low (trade against the technical trend; abort immediately if a candle closes beyond the tested extreme — don't wait for the stop). "Backside of the hour": H1/H4 candles reveal their true form only near the close — **act only on closed candles**.

### Pattern triggers / the trade catalogue
- **2nd-leg M/W (the core trade):** Asian range ≤50 pips → 3-push escape 25–50 pips beyond → M (top) or W (bottom) whose **second leg is less extreme than the first**, legs **30–90 min apart** (RRT = same thing faster). **Entry = close of the candle completing the 2nd leg.**
- **Straightaway:** trap move happened in a *previous* session/day (up to 2 days before NFP) + extended consolidation → true trend launches without a fresh stop hunt; identified by timing (after Level I consolidation), agreement with technicals, and a pullback to EMA 50 or 13.
- **Half Batman:** at Level I consolidation, no second leg back to the extreme (enough traders already trapped) — price just leaves. Never trade against the peak formation out of Level I consolidation.
- **33 trade:** day 3 of a 3-day rise **and** intraday Level III = strong sell (mirror for drops). Final consolidation may stretch and the day may close at the extreme — that strengthens, not weakens, the signal.
- **NYC reversal:** first ~3 hours from 08:00 ET; requires price at Level III, HOD/LOD already formed, ADR met/exceeded, price stretched from the EMAs, a reversal candle, RSI/TDI confirmation; target = EMA-50 or mid-range; expected yield 40–50 pips or less.
- **200 bounce ("Hold the Mayo"):** an M/W forming directly on the EMA-200 = highest-probability reversal.
- **Swing trade:** enter at the M/W that starts a new 3-day cycle; hold across levels (management in section 4).
- Full entry list given: M/W, straightaway, 33, swing, 200 bounce, 5/13 cross, pins/spikes, pivot holds/rejects, 3-level rise/drop, shark fin, half Batman, divergence, stop hunt, hourly close. **Exits are the same signals mirrored at the opposite extreme.**

### Codified pre-trade checklist (p.57)
1. Out of news time by ≥1 hour (Forex Factory). 2. Accumulation channel < 50 pips. 3. Count known. 4. Third-level triggers with M/W formation. 5. HOD/LOD locked in. 6. H1 close/trend agrees. 7. Pivot extreme (M1/M2 or M3/M4) reached. 8. TDI signals present. 9. Reversal candle formations present.
Cross-pair context ("fractional disparity"): pairs move levels in coordination — e.g., commodity currencies chopping at Level III while EUR/GBP/CHF run Level II means the crosses are being "worked"; a sharp move in one major ripples into its crosses within 1–2 candles. Scan set: EUR/GBP/AUD each vs USD, CHF, JPY on H1.

## 4. Risk and exit guidance in the PDF

- **Stop placement:** ideally above the first swing high after the push out of consolidation, because a **third swipe at the HOD/LOD is common**. Numerically: 2nd-leg M/W entries take a stop **7 pips beyond the more extreme leg** (7–10 pips outside the HOD/LOD); 1st-leg (early) entries need **23 pips** — sized as a 15-pip stop-run needle past the extreme + spread + margin. Add 10–15 pips of allowance to both SL and TP for imperfect tops/bottoms, and budget ~5 pips average spread + slippage per pair.
- **Targets:** initial TP ≈ **50 pips** from entry; NYC reversal 40–50 pips; on strong counts let it run level-to-level, trailing the stop behind the last completed consolidation zone. Level I/II legs average ~75 pips peak-to-consolidation. Pivot midpoints (M1/M2) serve as targets from a HOD entry.
- **Time-based exit (scratch rule):** if, **2 hours** after the 2nd leg of the M/W, the trade hasn't moved as expected — scratch it; volume was insufficient and an extended stop hunt (another ~25 pips) is likely; re-enter on the next setup at the next level. A stopped-out first attempt does not invalidate the pattern.
- **Failure economics:** with ADR ~150 pips and a 3-level day, repeatedly taking tight-stop entries with a fixed ~50-pip target eventually catches the true leg profitably even after 1–2 small losses.
- **Swing management:** after +50 pips move SL to breakeven; do **not** tuck the stop behind the Asian consolidation during Level I (the London-open stop hunt will take it); after the stop hunt passes, trail behind the Asian consolidation; only at Level III tighten and exit on the terminal stop hunt in the technical-trend direction (optionally reverse the position).
- **Scaling in (ratio 5:4:3:2:1):** fire order 2 once order 1 is in profit; at ~+20 pips move order 1 to BE; once order 4 is 10 pips clear, move the group stop between orders 3 and 4 ("now you cannot lose"); hold through midweek reversal for the 200-pip swing case.
- **Trailing stop, if unattended: 32 pips** (stated as empirically best for 50-pip targets).
- **Position sizing:** learning phase 1–3% risk per trade; "proficient" 5% (table: at $100k, a 10-pip stop = 10/30/50 lots for 1/3/5%). Frequency: 2–3 selective trades per week; income scales via size, not pip count.
- **Session discipline:** trade end-of-Asia → London for ~4 hours, break, then NY-open reversals 2–4 hours; enter during consolidations, never chase a running move (a chase inflates the stop and shrinks size); closed candles + multiple confirmations only; no setup = no trade.

## 5. Critical note — testable vs narrative

**Mechanically testable claims (directly implementable/falsifiable on M1–H1 data):**
- Asian range ≤50 pips → excursion terminating 25–50 pips beyond the range between 01:00–04:00 ET, in ≤3 pushes, ending in a pin — followed by a reversal that runs to the opposite side of the day. Measurable base rate.
- M/W with 30–90 min between legs, second leg not exceeding the first; 2-hour scratch window; 7/23-pip stops; 50-pip targets; 32-pip trail — a complete backtestable ruleset.
- HOD/LOD landing at pivot midpoints conditional on prior-day candle color; ADR-touch → NY-session reversal (08:00–11:00 ET); midweek (Tue–Wed) reversal; prior-day HOD/LOD spike-through-and-close-back-inside frequency.
- Spread widening at manipulation extremes and around releases — directly observable in tick data and already consistent with the team's "wycinki" observation.
- Pre-NFP: trap move up to 2 days before + consolidation into the release (Straightaway) — testable against the calendar.
- The 1-hour pre-news liquidity drought; post-news reversal of the pre-release drift ("trapped in line with the sentiment") — this is the team's core hypothesis and is statistically checkable per event class.

**Narrative / unfalsifiable elements (treat as story, not signal):**
- A single intentional "MM" entity; the IMF capping daily ranges; bank A→B→C circular trading as the cause of the Asian range; "offices" handing over manipulation duties; MM holidays causing December chop (the seasonality is testable, the attributed cause is not). "They know where your stops are" is true only for b-book internalizers, and is unobservable in market data either way.
- Accuracy claims ("better than 90% accurate," "90 or 95%" from mastering the count) — marketing-grade, unsupported by any data in the document.
- Pattern definitions carry many "acceptable variations" (more/fewer pushes, blown-out ranges, faster/slower M/W), which makes the full discretionary method prone to hindsight bias and overfitting — the notes themselves lean on hindsight training. An automated implementation must freeze one strict parameterization per pattern.
- The stated risk model (up to 5% per trade, 50 lots/$100k, aggressive pyramiding) is dangerous and directly conflicts with SkyTower guardrails (max $100/trade, $300/day) — adopt the *structure* (stop geometry, scratch timer, spread awareness), never the sizing.
- Era caveat: 2011 notes; hardcoded ET/GMT mappings ignore DST (relevant given the project's calendar-timezone lesson — re-anchor all windows in UTC), and MT4-era broker mechanics (requotes) are partly obsolete, though spread widening at events remains real.

**Bottom line for the team's news-fade system:** the PDF *supports the mechanism* (pre-move = trap; true move opposite; terminal stop-run wicks; spread games at the extreme; news used to finish/move levels) but *does not itself contain* a "fade the last pre-release M1 candle" rule — its explicit stance on news is avoidance (≥1h buffer, never NFP, no Fed-speech trading) plus an expert-level hint that news setups exist. The transferable, PDF-native ingredients for the automated strategy are: the location filter (where is price relative to Asian range ±25–50 pips, prior HOD/LOD, ADR, pivot midpoints, EMA 200 when the release hits), confirmation logic (close-based RSI divergence; band-recontainment as the stop-hunt-exhausted signal — noting the PDF's bands live on RSI, not price), wick-aware stop geometry (7–23 pips beyond the manipulation extreme, sized for a 15-pip needle), the 2-hour scratch timer, and fill/spread defenses at the release itself.
